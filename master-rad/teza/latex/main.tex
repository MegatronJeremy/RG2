\begin{abstract}
This thesis integrates four ray-traced lighting effects (shadows, ambient occlusion, reflections,
and diffuse global illumination) as inline ray-query (\texttt{VK_KHR_ray_query}) passes over a
rasterized forward renderer in a custom Vulkan engine. The noisy ray-traced signals (shadow visibility, ambient occlusion,
reflections, and global illumination) are reconstructed by a shared SVGF denoiser and stabilized by
temporal anti-aliasing. Real-time
rasterization approximates these global effects with screen-space and precomputed methods that fail
on off-screen or dynamic information; hardware ray tracing makes per-pixel scene queries feasible.
The per-effect GPU cost is isolated with an A/B configuration matrix, and reconstruction quality is
measured with FLIP, PSNR, and SSIM against a converged path-traced reference across several viewpoints. The identical
pipeline is compared across two vendors, AMD RDNA4 and NVIDIA Blackwell, isolating the effect of
hardware and driver on the same code. The two vendors rank the effects differently: shadows dominate
on the RX 9070 XT (2.272 ms) while on the RTX 5070 shadows, reflections, and global illumination cost
the same to within the measurement spread, for measured real-time totals of \perfTotalAMD{} ms and
\perfTotalNV{} ms respectively over a baseline under 2.3 ms. The choice of shadow technique is
likewise architecture-dependent, the stochastic producer being cheaper than per-light inline tracing
on AMD and 2.6 times more expensive on NVIDIA. Enabling all four effects lifts reconstruction quality
from FLIP 0.435 / PSNR 15.2 dB / SSIM 0.38 (raster) to FLIP 0.087 / PSNR 30.7 dB / SSIM 0.87
against the path-traced reference.
\end{abstract}
